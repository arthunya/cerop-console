Financial layer: three candidates

Each model was trained on the {{ facts.financial.train | int }} training firms and scored once on the {{ facts.financial.test | int }} test firms, at the default threshold of 0.50. Class imbalance was handled inside the models (class weights for Logistic Regression and Random Forest, a positive-class weight for XGBoost) rather than by inventing synthetic firms.

Recall, precision and F1 on the test set

Higher is better. Threshold 0.50.

Why XGBoost won

Logistic Regression catches the most bankruptcies, with recall of {{ finrep.logreg.recall | f3 }}, but its precision is {{ finrep.logreg.precision | f3 }}: most of the firms it flags are fine. An analyst would drown in false alarms. XGBoost has the best F1 ({{ finrep.xgb.f1 | f3 }}) and the best ROC-AUC ({{ finrep.xgb.roc_auc | f3 }}). Random Forest sits close behind on both, at {{ finrep.rf.f1 | f3 }} and {{ finrep.rf.roc_auc | f3 }}, which makes it a reasonable fallback.

Operations layer: three candidates

For late delivery the team compared Logistic Regression, a linear SVM and a Random Forest. Random Forest led on every metric that matters here. The linear models can't capture interactions such as shipping mode combined with order timing and region.
