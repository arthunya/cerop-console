The problem

Two risks that live with two different teams

A company buying across borders carries two separate worries. Will the supplier still be in business next quarter? Will this particular shipment arrive when it should? The first sits with credit or procurement. The second sits with logistics.

CEROP keeps those questions apart on purpose: two models, two scores. A solvent supplier can still ship late, and a failing one can ship on time right up to the end, so one blended number would hide what each team needs to see.

How it works

From raw data to a score you can question

Each layer goes through the same steps. The leakage checks and the threshold choice turned out to matter more than the choice of algorithm.

  1. DataTaiwan bankruptcy, DataCo orders, NY Fed GSCPI
  2. Cleaningsentinel values, IDs and free text out
  3. Leakage checksnothing known only after delivery
  4. Model selection3 candidates per layer
  5. Tuning & thresholdsearch, then calibrate
  6. Exportsaved once, loaded read-only
  7. Scoring + SHAPevery explanation adds up
  8. Consoletry it in the browser

My role

Arthunya Kanoklertwongse (Aty)

CEROP was a four-person team project. My parts:

  • Financial layer: exploring and cleaning the Taiwan bankruptcy data, including the sentinel-value tiers and capping rules.
  • Leakage verification: finding and checking the Order Status leakage in the operations data, and auditing the team's notebooks and numbers.
  • Live demo build: exporting the models, the scoring script, the SHAP explanations with their reconciliation checks, and the Risk Console.
